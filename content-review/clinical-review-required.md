## 1. Shockwave efficacy and tissue-regeneration language

- Proposed wording: “Extracorporeal shockwave therapy may be considered for selected tendinopathies after an evaluation. Outcomes vary by diagnosis, protocol, and comparator, and it should be presented as one option within a broader rehabilitation plan.”
- Pages: `/shockwave-therapy-nyc/`, `/focused-shockwave-vs-radial-shockwave/`, `/sports-injury-nyc/`, related homepage/service copy.
- Reason: current wording includes “research-backed,” “breaks through,” “strongest evidence,” “tissue regeneration,” and results that other care “can’t reach.”
- Evidence source: [PMID 37493215](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37493215/) reports no overall pain difference at 4 or 12 weeks across its comparator analysis; [PMID 38659004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38659004/) reports benefit across several tendinopathies with substantial heterogeneity.
- Strength of evidence: moderate but condition-specific and inconsistent across reviews.
- Risk of misunderstanding: readers may infer guaranteed benefit, superiority, or biologic regeneration.
- Clinician approval required: yes.

## 2. TECAR healing, fibrosis, and circulation claims

- Proposed wording: “TECAR uses capacitive and resistive radiofrequency energy and may be used as an adjunct for selected musculoskeletal presentations. Available studies suggest possible short-term pain and function benefits, but protocols and study quality vary.”
- Page: `/tecar-therapy-nyc/` and homepage technology summaries.
- Reason: current copy states that TECAR accelerates healing, reduces fibrosis, improves circulation, and clears inflammatory cytokines.
- Evidence source: [PMID 36698689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36698689/) reports possible pain effects with very high heterogeneity; [PMID 32909988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32909988/) found only 2 of 13 included studies at low risk of bias and called for further research.
- Strength of evidence: low to moderate; heterogeneous and protocol-dependent.
- Risk of misunderstanding: mechanistic statements can be read as proven clinical outcomes.
- Clinician approval required: yes.
